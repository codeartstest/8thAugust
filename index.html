History

Cambodia's history spans millennia, from the ancient Khmer Empire to modern nationhood. The Khmer Empire, which flourished from the 9th to the 15th century, was one of the most powerful empires in Southeast Asia. Its capital, Angkor, was the largest pre-industrial urban center in the world, home to the magnificent Angkor Wat temple complex.

In 1863, Cambodia became a French protectorate and later part of French Indochina. The colonial period lasted until 1953 when King Norodom Sihanouk secured independence through peaceful negotiations with France.

The 1970s brought devastating conflict, including the Khmer Rouge regime (1975-1979) under Pol Pot, which caused the deaths of an estimated 1.7 million people. Following the Vietnamese-backed overthrow of the Khmer Rouge, Cambodia gradually transitioned to a constitutional monarchy.

Today, Cambodia is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system. King Norodom Sihamoni has served as head of state since 2004. The country has experienced significant economic growth and development in the 21st century.

Geography

Cambodia is located in Southeast Asia, bordering Thailand to the west and northwest, Laos to the northeast, Vietnam to the east and southeast, and the Gulf of Thailand to the southwest. The country covers an area of approximately 181,035 square kilometers.

The landscape is dominated by the Mekong River, which flows through the country from north to south. The Mekong is the world's 12th longest river and is central to Cambodia's agriculture, transportation, and fishing industries.

Tonle Sap, the largest freshwater lake in Southeast Asia, is a UNESCO-designated biosphere reserve. It expands dramatically during the wet season, swelling to five times its dry-season size. This natural wonder supports millions of people through its rich fisheries.

The Cardamom Mountains in the southwest form the largest forested area in the country, while the Dangrek Mountains run along the northern border with Thailand. The central plains are ideal for rice cultivation, Cambodia's primary agricultural product.

Population

Cambodia has a population of approximately 17 million people as of 2024. The population is predominantly young, with a median age of around 26 years, reflecting the country's recovery from decades of conflict.

The vast majority of Cambodians, about 95%, are ethnic Khmer. The remaining population includes Cham (Muslim communities), Vietnamese, Chinese, and various indigenous hill tribes. This diversity adds cultural richness to Cambodian society.

Approximately 25% of the population lives in urban areas, with Phnom Penh, the capital, being the largest city with over 2 million residents. Other major cities include Siem Reap, Battambang, and Sihanoukville.

Buddhism is the state religion, practiced by approximately 97% of the population. Theravada Buddhism deeply influences Cambodian culture, daily life, and social values, with monks held in high regard throughout society.

Language

Khmer (or Cambodian) is the official language of Cambodia, spoken by over 16 million people. It belongs to the Mon-Khmer branch of the Austroasiatic language family, making it one of the oldest languages in Southeast Asia.

The Khmer script is derived from the Pallava script of southern India and is one of the oldest writing systems still in use. The script is abugida-based, with consonants carrying inherent vowels that are modified by subscripts and diacritics. Khmer has the longest alphabet among the world's writing systems, with 33 consonants and 24 vowels.

English is increasingly spoken, especially among younger generations and in urban areas. French, a legacy of colonial rule, is still spoken by some older Cambodians and in legal contexts.

Common Khmer phrases include:

  • Sua-s'dey — Hello
  • Arkoun — Thank you
  • Te nah teh? — Where is it?
  • Lee hi — Goodbye
